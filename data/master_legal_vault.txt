SECTION 63 BNS (formerly IPC Section 375) - Rape: definition and essential elements
Rape is defined by Section 63 Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS), subject to the statutory exceptions. The essential question is whether the sexual act occurred in circumstances covered by the section, including absence of free and valid consent or consent obtained through coercion, deception or incapacity as specified by law. Police must record information under Section 173 BNSS, preserve evidence, protect the survivor from intimidation, and arrange medical examination under Section 184 BNSS. Classification is subsection- and schedule-dependent; verify the current First Schedule and facts before stating bail or cognizable status.

SECTION 64(1) BNS (formerly IPC Section 376) - Rape: punishment
Rape under Section 64(1) BNS is punishable with imprisonment of not less than 10 years, which may extend to imprisonment for life, and fine, subject to the statutory text and applicable subsection. Rape is generally treated as cognizable and non-bailable, but police and courts must verify the precise subsection and current First Schedule. A survivor can report at any police station, including through Zero FIR under Section 173 BNSS, and should receive medical care and procedural support.

SECTION 70 BNS (formerly IPC Section 376D) - Gang rape
Section 70 BNS concerns gang rape where a woman is raped by one or more persons acting as a group or in furtherance of a common intention. The punishment is imprisonment of not less than 20 years, extendable to life imprisonment for the remainder of natural life, and fine, subject to the statutory provision. It is generally cognizable and non-bailable. Police should promptly register the information under Section 173 BNSS, secure the scene and digital evidence, arrange medical examination under Section 184 BNSS, and protect the survivor's identity and safety.

SECTION 74 BNS (formerly IPC Section 354) - Assault or criminal force to woman with intent to outrage modesty
Section 74 BNS covers assault or criminal force against a woman with intent to outrage or knowing it is likely to outrage her modesty. The offence is generally cognizable and non-bailable with punishment under the current statutory schedule. The exact classification depends on the applicable provision. The survivor may report at any police station under Section 173 BNSS and should be given safety, medical and investigation assistance.

SECTION 75 BNS (formerly IPC Section 354A) - Sexual harassment
Section 75 BNS covers specified acts of sexual harassment, including unwelcome physical contact and advances, demands or requests for sexual favours, showing pornography against a woman's will, and sexually coloured remarks, with punishment varying by the act. Police must record a cognizable complaint under Section 173 BNSS where applicable and investigate the facts. Verify the exact subsection, punishment and schedule before stating whether the offence is bailable.

POCSO SECTIONS 3 AND 4 - Penetrative sexual assault on a child
The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 (POCSO) protects every child below 18 years. Section 3 defines penetrative sexual assault and Section 4 provides punishment, subject to aggravated provisions and amendments. A POCSO report must be handled through the Special Juvenile Police Unit or local police, with child-friendly procedures, privacy and protection from intimidation. Section 19 requires reporting of a suspected offence; failure to report can attract statutory consequences. Bail, cognizable status and punishment must be checked against the precise POCSO section.

POCSO SECTIONS 7 AND 8 - Sexual assault without penetration
Section 7 POCSO defines sexual assault without penetration and Section 8 provides punishment. Section 11 addresses sexual harassment of a child and Section 12 provides punishment. Police must record and investigate information under the POCSO procedure, inform the Child Welfare Committee where required, arrange medical examination with consent and safeguards, and avoid exposing the child to the accused. Use the exact aggravated subsection before stating penalty or bail status.

SECTION 80 BNS (formerly IPC Section 304B) - Dowry death
Section 80 BNS concerns dowry death where the statutory conditions are met, including a woman's death by burns, bodily injury or otherwise than under normal circumstances within the specified period of marriage and cruelty or harassment for dowry soon before death. Punishment is imprisonment of not less than 7 years and may extend to life imprisonment. Police must promptly register information, conduct the required inquest and investigation, preserve evidence and follow magistrate and post-mortem requirements. The precise classification is generally cognizable and non-bailable, subject to the current schedule.

SECTION 85 BNS (formerly IPC Section 498A) - Cruelty by husband or relative
Section 85 BNS penalises cruelty by a husband or relative of the husband, with cruelty defined by Section 86 BNS. Punishment may extend to 3 years and fine. Police should record a cognizable complaint under Section 173 BNSS, assess immediate safety, preserve messages and medical records, and connect the survivor with protection and support services. Arrest is not automatic; Section 35 BNSS safeguards and necessity-of-arrest requirements apply. Verify the current schedule for bail classification.

SECTION 103 BNS (formerly IPC Section 302) - Murder
Section 103 BNS punishes murder with death or imprisonment for life and fine. The prosecution must establish the statutory ingredients and facts; a citizen should not be told that an accusation itself proves guilt. Murder is generally cognizable and non-bailable. Police should register information under Section 173 BNSS, secure the crime scene, preserve forensic and electronic evidence, arrange inquest and post-mortem, and produce an arrested person before a Magistrate within the Section 58 BNSS limit.

SECTION 109 BNS (formerly IPC Section 307) - Attempt to murder
Section 109 BNS concerns an act done with the intention or knowledge required for murder, where death does not occur. Punishment can extend to 10 years and fine, and the provision contains enhanced consequences where hurt is caused. It is generally cognizable and non-bailable. Police must record the FIR under Section 173 BNSS, obtain medical and forensic evidence, and investigate intent, weapon, injury and surrounding circumstances rather than relying on the label alone.

SECTION 115 BNS (formerly IPC Sections 323 and 324) - Voluntarily causing hurt
Section 115 BNS covers voluntarily causing hurt, with punishment depending on the applicable subsection and facts, including use of dangerous means where applicable. Police should document injuries through medical examination, preserve photographs and witness evidence, and record information under Section 173 BNSS when the offence is cognizable. Bailability and cognizability can differ by subsection, so check the current First Schedule.

SECTION 117 BNS (formerly IPC Section 325) - Voluntarily causing grievous hurt
Section 117 BNS covers voluntarily causing grievous hurt. Grievous hurt must match the statutory definition and medical evidence; punishment and classification depend on the subsection and circumstances. Police should arrange prompt medical examination, preserve treatment records and investigate the weapon, intent and injury. Do not infer a final offence section from the word assault alone.

SECTION 137 BNS (formerly IPC Section 363) - Kidnapping
Section 137 BNS addresses kidnapping, including taking or enticing a person from lawful guardianship or out of India as defined in the provision. The precise offence and punishment depend on the facts and subsection. Police should register information under Section 173 BNSS, circulate missing-person details, preserve CCTV and electronic evidence, and prioritise the person's safety. Classification and bail must be checked in the current schedule.

SECTION 303 BNS (formerly IPC Section 379) - Theft
Section 303 BNS covers dishonest moving of movable property out of another person's possession without consent. Punishment varies by subsection and circumstances, including the value and repeat-offence provisions. Police should record a complaint or FIR under Section 173 BNSS where the facts disclose a cognizable offence, preserve CCTV and device identifiers, and issue the appropriate recovery and seizure documentation. Check the First Schedule before stating bail status.

SECTION 304 BNS - Snatching
Section 304 BNS addresses snatching, including sudden, quick or forcible seizure of movable property from a person. The punishment and classification must be read with the current statutory text and First Schedule. Police should record information under Section 173 BNSS, secure CCTV and location evidence, issue an acknowledgement, and investigate the device or property identifiers. Immediate danger should be reported through 112.

SECTION 309 BNS (formerly IPC Sections 390 and 392) - Robbery
Section 309 BNS concerns robbery, which involves theft or extortion accompanied by violence, threat of instant violence, hurt or wrongful restraint as defined by law. Punishment can extend to 10 years and fine, with enhanced consequences in specified circumstances. It is generally cognizable and non-bailable. Police should register the FIR under Section 173 BNSS, secure the scene, preserve medical and CCTV evidence, and record seized property lawfully.

SECTION 310 BNS (formerly IPC Section 395) - Dacoity
Section 310 BNS concerns dacoity where five or more persons conjointly commit or attempt robbery. Punishment can extend to life imprisonment or rigorous imprisonment and fine, subject to the provision. It is generally cognizable and non-bailable. Police should promptly register information under Section 173 BNSS, identify suspects through lawful investigation, preserve scene and digital evidence, and follow search and seizure safeguards under Section 105 BNSS.

SECTION 318 BNS (formerly IPC Sections 415 and 420) - Cheating and fraud
Section 318 BNS covers cheating, including dishonest or fraudulent deception that induces delivery of property or an act or omission causing or likely to cause harm. Punishment varies by subsection; Section 318(4) applies to specified dishonest inducement to deliver property or valuable security. Police should preserve messages, payment records, account details and device evidence and record a cognizable complaint under Section 173 BNSS where applicable. Online financial fraud should also be reported immediately to 1930.

SECTION 35 BNSS - Arrest necessity and notice
Section 35 BNSS governs when police may arrest without warrant and requires recorded reasons and statutory safeguards. Where arrest is not required and the offence falls within the applicable limit, police should issue a notice to appear rather than arrest automatically. The person must comply with a lawful notice, and reasons for arrest or non-arrest must be handled according to the provision. Always check the exact offence, punishment and current BNSS text.

SECTIONS 38 AND 53 BNSS - Advocate access and medical examination after arrest
Section 38 BNSS provides the arrested person access to meet an advocate during interrogation, subject to statutory limits. Section 53 BNSS concerns medical examination of an arrested person by a registered medical practitioner and documentation of injuries. Police must maintain custody records and respect constitutional and statutory safeguards. These provisions do not themselves decide bail; that depends on the offence and court.

SECTION 173 BNSS - FIR, electronic information and Zero FIR
Section 173 BNSS permits information relating to a cognizable offence to be given orally or by electronic communication, subject to the statutory process. The police must reduce oral information to writing, read it over, obtain the informant's signature where required and provide a free copy. Zero FIR may be given at a police station without waiting for territorial jurisdiction; refusal can be followed by a written complaint to the Superintendent of Police and other remedies under law.

SECTION 184 BNSS - Medical examination of rape victim
Section 184 BNSS governs medical examination of a rape victim, with consent and statutory safeguards. Police should arrange prompt examination through a registered medical practitioner, preserve samples and records, and ensure dignity, privacy and access to treatment. Medical examination is an evidence and care process and is not a substitute for an FIR under Section 173 BNSS.

SECTION 105 BNSS - Audio-video recording of search and seizure
Section 105 BNSS requires audio-video recording of the process of search and seizure, including preparation and signing of the seizure list where the statutory conditions apply. Police should record the process lawfully, preserve the recording and provide required documentation to the occupant or affected person. Search powers, warrant requirements and exceptions depend on the facts and applicable provision.

SECTIONS 47 AND 48 BNSS - Grounds of arrest and family information
Sections 47 and 48 BNSS require communication of the grounds of arrest and information to a relative, friend or nominated person according to the statutory procedure. In a bailable case, the arrested person must be informed of the right to bail. Police should record the intimation and custody details. Section 58 BNSS limits detention before production before a Magistrate, excluding permitted journey time.

SECTION 58 BNSS - Production before Magistrate within 24 hours
Section 58 BNSS requires an arrested person to be taken before the nearest Magistrate within 24 hours, excluding necessary journey time, unless a lawful exception applies. Police must maintain custody records and seek remand through the Magistrate when further detention is legally requested. The exact remedy for breach depends on the facts and court process.

SECTION 185 MOTOR VEHICLES ACT, 1988 - Drunk driving
Section 185 of the Motor Vehicles Act concerns driving or attempting to drive with alcohol or specified drugs over the statutory limit or impairment threshold. The punishment differs for a first offence and a subsequent offence under the current Act and amendments. Police may conduct lawful testing and must follow procedure; do not drive after drinking. The exact penalty, compounding and licence consequences should be checked against current law and state implementation.

SECTION 134A MOTOR VEHICLES ACT - Good Samaritan protection
Section 134A of the Motor Vehicles Act protects a Good Samaritan who voluntarily assists an injured road accident victim, subject to statutory conditions. A helper should call 112 or emergency medical services, seek immediate care and preserve relevant information. Police and hospitals must follow the protections and procedures in the current law.

SECTION 66D INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY ACT, 2000 - Cheating by personation using computer resource
Section 66D IT Act concerns cheating by personation using a communication device or computer resource. Punishment can extend to 3 years and fine. Online financial fraud should be reported immediately to 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in so that banks and authorities can attempt fund tracing or freezing. Preserve UTR numbers, screenshots, phone numbers, messages and timestamps; BNS Section 318 may also apply depending on facts.

CYBER FINANCIAL FRAUD REPORTING - 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in
For an unauthorised UPI, card, wallet or online banking transaction, immediately call 1930, notify the bank or payment provider, and file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Provide transaction IDs, UTR, screenshots, beneficiary details, phone numbers and messages. Police may investigate under Section 66D IT Act and Section 318 BNS where the facts support those offences. Fund freezing is an urgent response process, not a guaranteed result.

LOST MOBILE OR DOCUMENT - Lost report, not automatically an FIR
If a mobile phone or document is merely lost without facts indicating theft, report it through the state police lost-property or Lost Document Report facility and obtain an acknowledgement; losing property alone is not automatically an offence. If there are facts of theft, report them under Section 303 BNS and Section 173 BNSS. Block the handset through the applicable CEIR or service-provider process and secure accounts and SIM access.

DRIVING DOCUMENTS - Documents required while driving
A driver should carry a valid driving licence, registration certificate, required motor insurance and valid Pollution Under Control certificate, with verifiable electronic records where accepted through DigiLocker or mParivahan. Commercial vehicles may require permit and fitness documents. Requirements can vary by vehicle and current state or RTO rules; Section 185 MVA concerns intoxicated driving, not the document list.

DOMESTIC VIOLENCE AND IMMEDIATE SAFETY
For cruelty by a husband or relative, Section 85 BNS and the definition in Section 86 BNS may apply when statutory ingredients are present. The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 may also provide civil protection, residence, monetary and custody remedies. Report a cognizable offence under Section 173 BNSS, seek immediate protection through 112, preserve messages and medical records, and contact a protection officer or legal aid service. The correct remedy depends on the facts and is not limited to one section.

EMERGENCY POLICE ASSISTANCE - 112
For immediate danger, violence, a road crash or a threat to life, call 112 and seek urgent medical help. Legal classification, bail status and investigation timelines must be confirmed from the current official law and case facts.
